Priorities

CrossCreek Church · Exodus 18:1–23 · October 25, 2026

Main Point: Following Christ requires prioritizing our time, talents, and gifts for God's glory rather than our own.

Lesson Introduction

Our culture is full of distractions, and hindsight is 20/20. Most of us can quickly think of ways we wish we had used our time differently. Time is limited, and we often fall into the trap of using it selfishly. As followers of Christ, God calls us to make the most of the time He has given us for His glory and kingdom (Ephesians 5:15–16; Matthew 6:33). In Exodus 18, Jethro challenges Moses to steward his time faithfully. The passage calls us to put God's glory above our own agendas and the good of the church above our own preferences.

Opening Questions

  • If you could start this week over, what would you do differently? What decisions would you change? How would you adjust your use of time?
  • What if you could start today over? What would you strive to get done? How would you adjust your priorities?
  • Was it difficult to think of answers to these questions? Why or why not?

Section 1: Remember God's Work (Exodus 18:1–12)

Read: Invite a volunteer to read Exodus 18:1–12.

Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, had been apart from Moses while caring for Moses' wife, Zipporah. When they reunited, Moses told Jethro how God had saved Israel from the Egyptians, protected the people, and provided for them.

Discuss

  • Why do you think Moses took time to tell Jethro what God had done to the Egyptians and how He delivered Israel? What might Moses have hoped to accomplish?
  • How did Jethro respond to Moses' report (vv. 9–12)? Given who Jethro was (v. 1), does this surprise you? Why or why not?
  • As we consider how to use our time, talents, and gifts for God's glory, why is it important to remember what God has done for us?
Did You Know? Jethro's response is both praise and worship

The passage identifies Jethro as priest of Midian. After hearing Moses recount God's deliverance, Jethro rejoices, blesses the Lord, says the Lord is greater than all gods (v. 11), and joins Moses, Aaron, and Israel's elders for a sacrificial meal (vv. 9–12). The lesson's central movement is from hearing about God's saving work to worship.

We do not know exactly which god or gods the Midianites worshiped. Jethro's response indicates that he recognized the Lord's unmatched power and grace. As we prioritize our time and resources for God and His kingdom, we first stop to consider His grace. Acknowledging God's glory leads us to devote ourselves wholly to Him.

Section 2: Share the Responsibility (Exodus 18:13–27)

Read: Invite a volunteer to read Exodus 18:13–27.

As God's chosen leader, Moses saw it as his duty to judge disputes among God's people. The task occupied him from morning until evening. Jethro pointed out that Moses' approach was not good: he was carrying too much alone, and the people had to wait for justice.

Discuss

  • In what ways did Moses' priorities need to change? Why did Jethro encourage him to share leadership responsibilities?
  • What message did Moses communicate by refusing to share leadership? What typically happens when one person tries to do it all?
  • What kind of help did Moses need in leading the people?
  • Looking at verse 21 and following, how were these leaders chosen? What were the prerequisites for their tasks? What are the implications for you and our church family?
Did You Know? Delegation preserved Moses' central responsibilities

Jethro's advice does not remove Moses from leadership. Moses is still to represent the people before God, teach them God's statutes and laws, and hear the most difficult cases (vv. 19–22). Other trustworthy leaders handle routine cases. The arrangement shares the work while keeping the responsibilities that require Moses' particular role.

Jethro counseled Moses to appoint assistants to handle less difficult cases. They were to be capable, fear God, and hate dishonest gain. The description highlights both ability and moral character. Many people are driven by profit, but the love of gain is dangerous for all of us, especially leaders.

Leader note: Keep the emphasis on shared service

The passage does not commend avoiding responsibility. Jethro's counsel helps Moses continue the work only he can do and equips other faithful people to serve. Connect this principle to 2 Timothy 2:2 and the church's mutual service.

By calling Moses to train other leaders and share the load, God, through Jethro, challenged Moses to recognize his human limitations. We are all limited in the time and resources we can invest in God's kingdom. That is why we need the church.

Section 3: One Body, Many Gifts (1 Corinthians 12:12–31)

Read: Invite a volunteer to read 1 Corinthians 12:12–31.

Discuss

  • What would Paul say to people in the church who refuse to share ministry burdens? What do we communicate to other members when we neglect to share those burdens?
  • Why is it important for believers with different gifts to come together in unity? What happens when they do not?

Paul reminds the church that God has given it diverse people with diverse gifts and abilities to supply what is lacking in the body. Every part of the human body is unique, and every part is essential. In the body of Christ, believers' gifts come together to build up the church. If we cannot come together in unity, what reason will the rest of the world have to believe our message? For the church to function rightly, every believer must know and embrace who God has made them to be in Christ.

Did You Know? The passage describes a tiered arrangement

Exodus 18:21–22 names leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. The text presents a layered system so that ordinary cases can be handled close to the people, while difficult cases can be brought to Moses. A recent scholarly study reads the episode through delegation and shared communal responsibility; see the source listed below.

Application

Help your group identify how the truths from Scripture apply directly to their lives.

  • Based on today's study, what specific ways might God be calling you to rethink your priorities?
  • How might we, as a group, help each other prioritize God's glory over our own and His kingdom over our agendas?
  • What talents and abilities are represented in your group? How can we use these gifts together to build the church and serve our community?

Prayer

Close in prayer, thanking God for the diversity in our church. Ask God to help us see both our gifts and the gifts of others so that we might work together to glorify Him and build His kingdom.

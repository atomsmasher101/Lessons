Priorities

CrossCreek Church · Exodus 18:1–23 · October 25, 2026

Main Point: Following Christ requires prioritizing our time, talents, and gifts for God's glory rather than our own.

Introduction

As your group time begins, use this section to introduce the topic of discussion.

Opening Discussion

  • If you could start this week over, what would you do differently? What different decisions would you make? How would you adjust your use of time?
  • What if you could start today over? What would you strive to get done? How would you adjust your priorities?
  • Was it difficult for you to think of answers to the above two questions? Why or why not?

We live in a culture full of distractions, and hindsight is 20/20. The fact that most of us can readily think of ways we wish we could spend our time differently shows that we all have a time-management problem. Time is limited. We will never get it back, and because we are sinners, we often fall into the trap of using it selfishly. As followers of Christ, God calls us to make the most of the time He has given us for His glory and kingdom (Ephesians 5:15–16; Matthew 6:33). God calls us to prioritize our time and resources. Looking at Exodus 18 and how Jethro challenged Moses to steward his time more faithfully, we are challenged to prioritize God's glory over our own agenda and the good of the church over our own desires.

Understanding

Unpack the biblical text to discover what Scripture says and means about this topic.

Read Exodus 18:1–12

Invite a volunteer to read the passage.

  • Why do you think Moses took time to tell Jethro what God had done to the Egyptians and how He delivered the Israelites? What did Moses hope to accomplish?
  • How did Jethro respond to Moses' report (vv. 9–12)? Given who Jethro was (v. 1), does this surprise you? Why or why not?
  • As we consider how to use our time, talents, and gifts for God's glory, why is it important to remember what God has done for us?

We do not know exactly which god or gods the Midianites worshiped. Jethro's response to Moses' account, however, indicates that he, as priest of Midian, may have worshiped other gods. After Moses testified to God's miraculous and gracious deliverance of Israel from Egypt, Jethro confessed that “Yahweh is greater than all gods” (v. 11). When he became aware of God's power and grace in the lives of Moses and the Israelites, he confessed the Lord as God and worshiped Him. As we prioritize our time and resources for God and His kingdom, we first consider His grace and power. Acknowledging God's unmatched glory leads us to devote ourselves wholly to Him.

Read Exodus 18:13–27

Invite a volunteer to read the passage.

  • In what ways did Moses' priorities need to change? Why did Jethro encourage Moses to share his leadership responsibilities?
  • What message did Moses communicate by refusing to share leadership? What typically happens when one person tries to do it all?
  • What kind of help did Moses need in leading the people?
  • Looking at verse 21 and following, how were these leaders chosen? What were the prerequisites for their tasks? What are the implications for you and our church family?

As God's chosen leader, Moses saw it as his duty to judge disputes among God's people. The task occupied him from morning until evening. Jethro pointed out that this method was not good: Moses was trying to do too much and needed to share leadership with other faithful men (see 2 Timothy 2:2). Christians can spread themselves too thin when they think too highly of their own abilities and neglect the gifts and contributions of others.

Jethro counseled Moses to appoint assistants to handle less difficult cases. They were to be capable people of strength, valor, and virtue; fear God; and hate dishonest gain. Moral character mattered. Love of gain is dangerous for everyone, especially leaders.

By calling Moses to train other leaders and share the load, God, through Jethro, challenged Moses to recognize his human limitations. We are all limited in the time and resources we can invest in God's kingdom. That is why we need the church. In 1 Corinthians 12, Paul reminds us that God has given the church people with diverse gifts and abilities to supply what is lacking in the body.

Read 1 Corinthians 12:12–31

Invite a volunteer to read the passage.

  • What would Paul say to people in the church who refuse to share ministry burdens? What do we communicate to other church members when we neglect to share those burdens?
  • Why is it important for believers with different gifts to come together in unity? What happens when they do not?

Every part of the human body is unique, and every part is essential for the body to do what it was designed to do. In the body of Christ, believers' gifts come together to build up the church. If we cannot come together in unity, what reason will the rest of the world have to believe the message we tell them? For the church to function rightly, every believer must know and embrace who God has made them to be in Christ.

Application

Help your group identify how the truths from Scripture apply directly to their lives.

  • Based on today's study, what specific ways might God be calling you to rethink your priorities?
  • How might we, as a group, help each other prioritize God's glory over our own and His kingdom over our agendas?
  • What talents and abilities are represented in your group? How can we use these gifts together to build the church and serve our community?

Prayer

Close in prayer, thanking God for the diversity in our church. Ask God to help us see both our gifts and the gifts of others so that we might work together to glorify Him and build His kingdom.

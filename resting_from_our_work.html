Resting from Our Work

CrossCreek Church · Exodus 31:12–17; Hebrews 4:8–13 · November 8, 2026

Main Point: Observing a Sabbath rest helps us keep work in its proper place, and it can make our six days of labor more productive.

Lesson Introduction

What we do is not who we are, yet work can very easily become our identity and even our god. God has created us with work to do, but He has set clear parameters for that work. Observing a Sabbath rest helps us keep work in its proper place and can make our six days of work more productive.

Opening Discussion

  • Complete this sentence: “I wish I had more time to...”
  • Based on the events of an average week, is rest something you value? Why is it so hard for us to find time to rest?

Section 1: Sabbath as a Sign (Exodus 31:12–17)

Read the passage together.

12The Lord spoke to Moses, saying, 13“But as for you, speak to the sons of Israel, saying, ‘You shall surely observe My sabbaths; for this is a sign between Me and you throughout your generations, that you may know that I am the Lord who sanctifies you. 14Therefore you are to observe the sabbath, for it is holy to you. Everyone who profanes it shall surely be put to death; for whoever does any work on it, that person shall be cut off from among his people. 15For six days work may be done, but on the seventh day there is a sabbath of complete rest, holy to the Lord; whoever does any work on the sabbath day shall surely be put to death. 16So the sons of Israel shall observe the sabbath, to celebrate the sabbath throughout their generations as a perpetual covenant.’ 17It is a sign between Me and the sons of Israel forever; for in six days the Lord made heaven and earth, but on the seventh day He ceased from labor, and was refreshed.”

🏛️ Did You Know? The Sabbath was a covenant sign
Exodus calls the Sabbath a sign between the Lord and Israel throughout their generations (vv. 13, 16–17). The passage connects the weekly day of rest with both God's covenant relationship and His work as Creator.

Discussion: The Purpose of Sabbath

  • What is the purpose of the Sabbath? What do you think God meant by keeping the Sabbath day holy?
  • What do you find most challenging when you think about a day of rest?
  • Why does rest matter to God? How did He demonstrate this for us?
  • How was practicing Sabbath rest an exercise in trust? What do we communicate to God when we fail to rest?

The Sabbath command is given at length so Israel will understand its nature and purpose. Taking one day each week to stop work and worship God can be difficult. Whether living on the edge of survival or in plenty, people are tempted to work furiously to get more for themselves. God called Israel to dedicate one day each week to remember that He supplies their needs and to deepen their relationship with Him.

We, like the Israelites, need work. Work develops discipline, helps us contribute to society, and brings satisfaction. Yet work does not need to consume every moment. Our bodies, minds, and souls need refreshment through time with God.

Lesson Insight: Sabbath rest trains us to trust God's provision
God's rest on the seventh day is presented as a pattern for His people. Rather than believing they supply their own needs through continuous work, Israel was to focus on the One who sustains life. Sabbath rest interrupts the drive to accumulate more and turns attention to the God who provides abundant life.

The Sabbath day was a critical part of God's covenent with Israel. He wanted them to cease working but also to keep the day holy. Holiness meaning set apart. It wasn't just another day they happpened to not work on. It was a holy day of rest. One to be regarded and meant to remember God's provision.

Reflect

  • In what ways do you feel as if you are living on autopilot? How do your habits and routines keep you from resting?
  • Why might our ability or inability to rest be more about what we believe than the commitments on our schedule?

Section 2: Sabbath Is for People (Mark 2:27)

Read Jesus’ words:

27Jesus said to them, “The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath.”

Discuss

  • How should we live in response to this verse today?
Lesson Insight: Rest is a gift ordered toward life with God
By saying, "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath", Jesus was restating the principle that the Sabbath rest was instituted to relieve man of his labors, just as He came to relieve us of our attempting to achieve salvation by our works. We no longer rest for only one day, but forever cease our laboring to attain God’s favor.

Section 3: Entering God's Rest (Hebrews 4:8–16)

Read the passage together.

8For if Joshua had given them rest, He would not have spoken of another day after that. 9So there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God. 10For the one who has entered His rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from His. 11Therefore let us be diligent to enter that rest, so that no one will fall, through following the same example of disobedience. 12For the word of God is living and active and sharper than any two-edged sword, and piercing as far as the division of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart. 13And there is no creature hidden from His sight, but all things are open and laid bare to the eyes of Him with whom we have to do. 14Therefore, since we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let’s hold firmly to our confession. 15For we do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but One who has been tempted in all things just as we are, yet without sin. 16Therefore let’s approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace for help at the time of our need.

Discussion: The Promise of Rest

  • Earlier in chapter 4, the writer of Hebrews described the Israelites' failure to enter God's rest (see Hebrews 4:6). Why did they fail? What does that have to do with our inability to rest today?
  • What might an inability to rest in God's mercy and grace show you about where you place your self-worth?
  • How might a lack of Sabbath rest influence the way you interact with other people?
  • One benefit of regularly practicing Sabbath rest is that it helps us keep our focus on serving God rather than trying to appear to be a good person. Have you experienced Sabbath helping you in this way? If so, share.
  • Verse 15 says that we do not have a high priest who cannot sympathise with our weaknesses, but One who has been tempted in all things just as we are, yet without sin. Does this make you feel encouraged or intimidated? How does this verse help us "approach the throne of grace with confidence"?
🏛️ Did You Know? “Sabbath rest” is a distinctive word in Hebrews
Hebrews 4:9 uses the Greek word sabbatismos, translated here “Sabbath rest.” This word appears only here in the New Testament and emphasizes the special significance of sharing God's own rest.

The Lord had promised Israel a rich land, but the people did not believe His promises. Because of their lack of faith, they failed to rest in the land and wandered for forty years. God wanted them to rest in his ability to provide istead of relying on their own strength and fleeing because of their inadequacy. Life can feel like that when we do not rest—an endless cycle of running without getting anywhere. True rest is not always about sleep; it is also about completion. God was not tired when He rested from creation; His work was finished, and He could enjoy it. God's work on the cross is finished as well and now is the time to enjoy it.

Lesson Insight: The finished work of Christ frees us from proving ourselves
The cross is the foundation of Christian rest. Because of Christ, we are valued by God and need not secure our future or prove ourselves to others. In Christ, we can work while resting in what God has already done for us. Our labor becomes a response of gratitude rather than an attempt to earn significance.

When we are in Christ, we can live in a state of rest even when physically tired. The finished work of God transforms the way we work: we do not work to prove ourselves or secure the future; we work in celebration of what God has already done for us in Christ.

Application

Help your group identify how the truths from Scripture apply directly to their lives.

Apply the Lesson

  • Based on our study today, how does Sabbath rest differ from your typical view of rest?
  • How would your life need to change in order for you to regularly practice Sabbath rest? How can we hold each other accountable?
  • What is one practical way you can help your family learn Sabbath rest? What do you hope will happen as a result?

Prayer

For your closing prayer time, practice rest and silence. Allot at least 10 minutes at the end of your group meeting to spend in quiet, reflective prayer.

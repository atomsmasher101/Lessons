Priorities: Teaching Guide

CrossCreek Church · Exodus 18:1–23 · October 25, 2026

Main Point: Following Christ requires prioritizing our time, talents, and gifts for God's glory rather than our own.

Suggested 45-minute plan

  1. 0–7 minutes · Introduction: Ask the opening questions about redoing the week or day. Connect the answers to distractions, limited time, and the call to use time for God's glory (Ephesians 5:15–16; Matthew 6:33).
  2. 7–18 minutes · Exodus 18:1–12: Read together. Discuss Moses' testimony, Jethro's response, and how remembering God's past grace shapes faithful priorities.
  3. 18–32 minutes · Exodus 18:13–27: Read together. Explore Moses' workload, Jethro's counsel, shared leadership, and the character of those chosen to serve.
  4. 32–40 minutes · 1 Corinthians 12:12–31: Read together. Connect shared leadership to the church's diverse gifts and mutual dependence.
  5. 40–45 minutes · Application and prayer: Name a practical priority to change, identify gifts in the group, and pray for unity and service.

Adjust the timing to fit your gathering. Keep the discussion focused on the main point: God's glory and the good of the church take priority over self-directed agendas.

Exodus 18:13–27 · Commentary

Verses 13–14 · The burden of judging

In the ancient Near East, judging civil and criminal disputes was part of leadership. Moses had likely judged regularly, but difficult cases may have remained unresolved while Israel was on the move. Now he spent the daylight hours hearing cases and was apparently still working at evening. Jethro recognized that this workload could not continue indefinitely. Nothing was wrong with Moses serving as judge; the problem was serving as the only judge for both simple and complex cases.

Moses may not have been acting foolishly or overconfidently. He may have been waiting for God to reveal a suitable judicial system. Moses was also a prophet who conveyed God's revelation, which helps explain why he believed he needed to judge the people himself.

Verses 15–16 · Moses' prophetic role

Moses explained that the people came to him to inquire of God. He decided between parties and made known God's decrees and laws. The process involved seeking God's decision, and Moses understood himself to be the conduit through whom it was given.

Verses 17–18 · A burden for Moses and the people

The workload was too much for one man: Moses and the people waiting for justice could not finish in a reasonable time. Moses gave himself wholeheartedly to the work, yet became trapped by the caseload. Despite good intentions, the system had bogged down, something Jethro could see as a newcomer to Israel's situation.

Verses 19–20 · Preserve Moses' essential work

Jethro did not ask Moses to stop judging, representing the people's concerns to God, or teaching God's decrees and laws. The people needed someone to show them how to live and what duties to perform, and Moses remained responsible for that instruction. Jethro offered his counsel as a proposal to be confirmed by God, not as a claim to divine authority.

Verses 21–22 · Select trustworthy leaders

Jethro proposed a trustworthy judicial hierarchy. The judges were to be capable people who feared God and hated dishonest gain. They would be appointed as officials rather than inherit office automatically. Leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens could handle routine cases, leaving only the most difficult matters for Moses. This gave the people regular access to justice and freed Moses to focus on prayer, worship, teaching, and preaching God's laws.

Verse 23 · Seek God's confirmation

Jethro did not presume to reorganize Israel's judiciary on his own authority. He expected Moses to ask God whether the plan represented His will. The intended result was relief for both Moses and the people.

Verses 24–26 · The plan is carried out

The text summarizes Moses' implementation of Jethro's counsel. Though not stated explicitly, it may be inferred that Moses learned from God that the advice was acceptable. Israel established a hierarchical court system with chosen leaders, and Moses served as the highest court for cases that could not be resolved below.

Leader notes and discussion prompts

Read Exodus 18:1–12

  • Invite participants to notice that Moses begins by recounting God's deliverance. Ask what remembering and retelling God's work does to our priorities.
  • Jethro responds with joy, praise, and worship. Highlight that prioritizing God begins with recognizing His grace and unmatched glory.

Read Exodus 18:13–27

  • Ask what was unsustainable about Moses' approach and what Jethro's advice preserved: Moses' teaching and his role in difficult cases.
  • Discuss the qualifications for leaders: ability, reverence for God, and honesty. Ask how these qualities protect the community.
  • Emphasize shared responsibility as faithful stewardship, not withdrawal from service. Connect to 2 Timothy 2:2.

Read 1 Corinthians 12:12–31

  • Invite the group to name gifts and abilities present among them and consider how they can serve together.
  • Ask what refusing to contribute communicates to the rest of the body, and how diverse gifts strengthen the church's witness.

Application and closing prayer

Ask each participant to identify one concrete way to reprioritize time or share a ministry responsibility. As a group, name gifts represented and one way to use them to build the church and serve the community.

Prayer: Thank God for the diversity in the church. Ask Him to help the group recognize its own gifts and the gifts of others, working together to glorify Him and build His kingdom.
